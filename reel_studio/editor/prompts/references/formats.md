# Formats: story shapes

Pick the format from the brief, or from the footage. Set `"format"` in the edit list; the renderer
warns when the cut's length falls outside the format's target (the first range below; the second
range is a short-cut guide, not used by the renderer). Lengths are defaults [I]; the order's target length wins;
brand Reels of 45-60 s had the best engagement in Socialinsider's 2026 data, but never pad:
a strong 25 s beats a padded 45 s. Make ONE cut per Reel.

## recipe — cooking or baking from scratch (target 30-50 s; short cut 15-25 s)
1. Hook: finished food in motion (break, pull, pour, slice), 1.5-3 s. Hook text.
2. Process in true recipe order. One action per shot, cut mid-action, max 2 s each.
   Show what changes (melting, combining, rising), skip what does not (an empty bowl, a hand
   reaching). Group repeated actions: one flour drop, not three.
3. Transformation moment (oven out, flip, sauce thickens) as a mini-peak around 60-75%.
4. Payoff: the best version of the money shot (inside, first bite, pull). Ends where the hook
   began, so the loop restarts smoothly.
Labels: key step names and amounts you read on screen/packaging. 4-8 labels.

## dessert / treat machine — e.g. Ninja Creami, waffle maker, air fryer (target 20-40 s; short cut 10-20 s)
1. Hook: the texture shot (spoon dragged through ice cream, scoop, drip), slow motion if 60 fps.
2. Ingredients fast, 0.6-1 s each. Freeze/wait steps collapse into one sped-up shot or a cut.
3. The machine moment: lid off, spin, the reveal of texture. This is the peak.
4. Payoff: spoon or bite, close. Practical value text if seen: protein, calories, minutes.

## restaurant — review or visit (target 20-40 s; short cut 10-20 s)
1. Hook: the best dish moment (cheese pull, cut, pour, steam) or a verdict question as text.
2. Context fast: sign or entrance 0.8 s, room 0.8 s (skip if footage is weak).
3. Dishes: each dish gets its best moment, 1-2 s. Order: good, better, best.
4. Reaction or verdict: hand reaching back for more, empty plate, rating as text ONLY if given.
5. Payoff: the best dish again, or the verdict. Name, area and price go in the caption and in
   text ONLY if the brief or footage gives them. Faces: keep only if the brief allows.
A text version can carry verdict text; the clean version is where a voiceover can go later.

## product — showing a product or gadget in use (target 20-40 s; short cut 10-20 s)
Hook on the result the product creates, then 3-5 fast "how it works" shots, then the result
again. Never show a brand claim you cannot see; name products only if visible or briefed.

## compilation — several foods, places or moments (target 15-35 s; short cut 8-18 s)
Best item first, then a rising sequence, best-but-one last. Each item 1-2 s at its peak moment.
Consistent rhythm matters more than order. Optional list text: "5 cookies in Madrid".

## asmr — sound-led (target 15-40 s; short cut 8-20 s)
Real speed for every sound shot. Fewer, longer shots allowed (set allow_long with a reason).
Labels minimal or none. The loudest, cleanest sound is the hook.

## pov — first-person mini story (target 10-30 s; short cut 7-15 s)
Hook text carries the situation ("POV: ..."). Fast, handheld feel is fine. Ends on a funny or
satisfying beat.

## what-i-eat — a day of food (target 25-45 s; short cut 12-25 s)
Time-of-day labels ("8 am"), one hero shot per meal, best meal first as the hook, then in order.

## food-fitness — food with training (target 15-35 s; short cut 8-18 s)
Contrast is the hook: the treat vs the workout, or the high-protein angle. Keep training
casual and fun, not coaching. Text carries the numbers only if given (protein, minutes).

## other
Fall back to: strongest moment first, rising middle, payoff that loops.

## Voiceover later
Clean versions are the voiceover base. When a voiceover exists in future, the cut stays and
the narration goes on top; keep hook moments 1.5-3 s so a sentence fits.
