# Structure: talk

A person tells the viewer one thing worth hearing. The story is the argument, not the footage.

1. Hook (0-3 s): the strongest spoken line, or the claim the talk will prove, on the best face
   or food shot. If the speaker's own first line is weak, open on the payoff line and come back.
2. Point: the speaker's main point in their own words, in the clearest take, one phrase per
   segment, with food or hands cut in over the voice to keep the picture moving.
3. Proof (about 60-75 % in): the moment that shows what was said (the bite, the result, the
   reaction), with the voice running over it or a short pause for the sound.
4. Close: the last line that lands (a verdict, a tip, a question), ending on a frame that
   loops back into the hook.
Keep at most one idea per segment. Leave out tangents, even good ones.
